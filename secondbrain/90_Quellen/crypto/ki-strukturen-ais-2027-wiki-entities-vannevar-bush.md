# Vannevar Bush

**Type:** Entity (Historical)

**Quellen:** *s02_llm_wiki*

## Zusammenfassung

Vannevar Bush (1890-1974), Ingenieur und Wissenschaftler, der 1945 das **Memex-Konzept** vorschlug — eine persönliche, kuratierte Wissensdatenbank mit assoziativen Verknüpfungen zwischen Dokumenten. Dies ist der historische Vorläufer zu modernen *c05_kb_design* Systemen und *s02_llm_wiki*.

## Das Memex Konzept (1945)

### Original Vision
Bush schrieb einen einflussreichen Essay "As We May Think" der eine hypothetische Maschine beschrieb:

**Memex Features:**
- Personal, desktop-sized knowledge store
- Curated collection of documents
- **Associative trails** between documents (Links!)
- User can create custom connections
- Records user's research process

### Kernidee
Nicht hierarchische klassifikation (Dewey Decimal) sondern **associative trails**:
- "When I follow a trail to concept X, show me related Y, Z"
- Connections als wertvoll als die Dokumente selbst
- Personal, private curation

## Historical Context

**Zeit (1945):** Post-WWII, wissenschaftliche Information explodiert

**Problem Bush sah:**
- Too much published research
- Humans can't keep up
- Need personalized knowledge system
- Need associative, non-hierarchical organization

**His Solution:** Memex (hypothetical machine)

---

## Memex vs Web

### Original Memex Vision
✓ Personal, private  
✓ Curated (user chooses sources)  
✓ Associative trails preserved  
✓ User at center  
✓ Maintained over time

### What Web Actually Became
✗ Public, open (lost curation)  
✗ Hierarchy lost to search ranking  
✗ Associative trails became links (but deteriorate)  
✗ Algorithm at center (not user)  
✗ Passive consumption (not maintenance)

**Quote from *s02_llm_wiki*:**
> "The idea is related in spirit to Vannevar Bush's Memex (1945) — a personal, curated knowledge store with associative trails between documents. Bush's vision was closer to this than to what the web became: private, actively curated, with the connections between documents as valuable as the documents themselves. **The part he couldn't solve was who does the maintenance.** The LLM handles that."

---

## The Missing Piece: Maintenance

Memex had one unfixable problem in 1945:
- Who updates the connections?
- Who maintains consistency?
- Who curates the stream?

**Answer in 1945:** Human curator does it full-time (impractical)  
**Answer in 2026:** LLM Agent does it (near-zero cost)

---

## Modern Parallels

### Memex → Personal Wikis (90s-2000s)
- Wikis like TiddlyWiki, Roam Research
- Users tried to be curators + maintainers
- Failed at scale (maintenance burden)

### Memex → PKM Tools (2020s)
- Obsidian, Logseq, Notion
- Great for **structuring** knowledge
- Still require humans to do maintenance
- Better but still burdensome

### Memex → LLM Wikis (2025+)
- Combines Obsidian/structure with LLM-automation
- LLM does the curation + maintenance + cross-referencing
- Finally solves Bush's unsolvable problem

## Legacy

Bush's influence:
- Inspired hypertext research (Nelson, Engelbart)
- Inspired early Web visionaries
- Still relevant to modern knowledge work
- **Especially relevant now with LLMs**
